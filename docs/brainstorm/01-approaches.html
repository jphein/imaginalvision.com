Three Approaches for imaginalvision.com

Landing page structure — pick the one that feels right

A

Portal Hub — Equal Weight

A beautiful entry page with the Imaginal Vision brand at top, then three cards linking to each offering: Art Cards, Dreamspace, and Creative Sight Counseling. Minimal text — mostly visual. Think of it like a gallery entrance with three doors.

Pros

  • Clean, simple, fast to build
  • Each project gets equal presence
  • Easy to add new offerings later

Cons

  • Doesn't tell the story — just a menu
  • Visitors need to click again to learn anything
  • Weaker for SEO (thin content)
B

Narrative Scroll — Tools as Story

A single-page scroll that tells the story: who Cassandra is → what the imaginal means → the tools she's built (Art Cards, Dreamspace) → who they're for (clients, students, fellow therapists) → Creative Sight Counseling practice info → contact. The domain proposal page's style, but as a real site.

Pros

  • Tells the full story — visitors understand the vision
  • Great for SEO (rich content, keywords)
  • Builds trust before asking people to try tools
  • Works for all 4 audiences (clients, students, therapists, prospects)

Cons

  • More content to write and maintain
  • Longer to build
  • Risk of being too long if not curated well
C

Hero + Cards — Quick Impact

A bold hero section with "Imaginal Vision — Expressive Art Therapy & Digital Tools" and Cassandra's tagline, followed immediately by two featured tool cards (Art Cards, Dreamspace) with descriptions and "Try it" buttons. Below that, a compact "About" section and link to Creative Sight Counseling. One viewport to understand, two clicks to engage.

Pros

  • Fast to grasp — tools are front and center
  • Gets people into the tools quickly
  • Balanced: not too thin, not too long

Cons

  • Less room for the philosophical depth (imaginal lineage)
  • Counseling practice is secondary

My recommendation: B (Narrative Scroll). The name "Imaginal Vision" demands explanation — it's a feature, not a bug. The story IS the brand. Once people understand the lineage and the tools, they'll engage more deeply. And it gives you rich content for SEO when therapists and students search for expressive arts tools.
